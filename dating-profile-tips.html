How to write a dating profile people want to reply to

Photos, prompts and a short bio that give people something to say.

On this page

  1. Choose your photos
  2. Answer prompts with a story
  3. Write a short bio
  4. Be honest about what you want
  5. Common mistakes

Choose your photos

  • Start with a clear photo of your face, on your own, in good light. No sunglasses, no group shot.
  • Add a full-length photo and a couple that show what you do: cooking, travelling, playing a sport, with your pet.
  • Keep them recent. Photos from the last year or two. People notice when someone looks different in person.
  • Every photo should be a conversation starter. A place, a hobby or a moment gives people an easy first message.

Answer prompts with a story

Short, specific answers beat long lists. Compare:

  • Weak: “I love travel, food and music.”
  • Better: “I once took a 14-hour train to Hampi for one sunrise. Worth it.”

The second gives someone a question to ask. Aim for answers people can reply to: a small story, an opinion, a habit, a plan.

Write a short bio

Two or three lines is enough. Say what you do, one thing you love, and one thing you’re looking for in the other person. Skip the list of what you don’t want — it reads as negative and puts good people off.

Be honest about what you want

Whether it’s a life partner, something long-term or still figuring it out, say so. Being clear saves everyone time and brings you people who want the same thing. On Bloomlet you can choose this in your profile basics.

Common mistakes

  • Only group photos, so nobody knows which one is you.
  • An empty bio, or one line like “Ask me”.
  • Old or heavily filtered photos.
  • Negativity: “No time-wasters”, “Don’t message if…”.
  • Sharing your phone number, workplace or home area on your profile. Keep those for later. See our safety tips.
