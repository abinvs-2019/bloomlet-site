First messages that get replies

How to open a conversation on a dating app without “hi” or a copied line.

Why most first messages fail

“Hi”, “Hey, how are you?” and a single emoji are the most common first messages on any dating app, and the easiest to ignore. They give the other person nothing to answer. Lines copied from the internet aren’t much better: people have seen them before, and they say nothing about you.

The one rule: make it about them

The messages that get replies show you actually read their profile. Pick one specific thing — a photo, an answer to a prompt, an interest — and say something or ask something about it. Specific beats clever every time.

That’s how Bloomlet works by design: you say hi with a flower attached to one thing on their profile, so your first message already has something real to talk about.

25 first message ideas

About a photo

  • “Where was this taken? It looks like the kind of place I keep saying I’ll go to.”
  • “Okay, the dog in your third photo — name, please.”
  • “Is that a trek or a very ambitious walk? I need the story.”
  • “That street food looks serious. Which city, and is it worth the trip?”
  • “Concert photo! Who were you seeing?”

About a prompt or something they wrote

  • “You said your perfect Sunday is a long breakfast. Masala dosa or something fancier?”
  • “I have to ask about the ‘unpopular opinion’ answer. Defend it.”
  • “You wrote you’re learning the guitar. How long before your first song?”
  • “Your answer about your family made me smile. Are you the planner of the group?”
  • “‘Will travel for good coffee’ — what’s the best cup you’ve found so far?”

About an interest you share

  • “Another person who reads before bed! What are you on right now?”
  • “You’re into badminton too. Morning player or evening player?”
  • “Panchayat fan? Which season is your favourite?”
  • “A fellow cook. What’s the one dish you’re proud of?”
  • “You like trekking — any trail you’d recommend for a beginner?”

Light questions that are easy to answer

  • “Mountains or beaches, and you can’t say both.”
  • “What’s the best thing you ate this week?”
  • “Chai or coffee? This decides a lot.”
  • “What’s something you’re looking forward to this month?”
  • “If you had a free Saturday tomorrow, what would you do?”

Playful, not cheesy

  • “I was going to send a pickup line, but your profile deserved better.”
  • “Rate my guess: you’re the friend who always knows a good place to eat.”
  • “Two truths and a lie, you go first.”
  • “Settle this: is pineapple on pizza okay?”
  • “Honest question: how many of your plants are still alive?”

What to avoid

  • Comments only about their looks. A compliment is fine; a first message that’s only about their body isn’t.
  • Long paragraphs. One or two lines is enough. Leave them something to say.
  • Asking for their number straight away. Talk in the app first. It’s safer for both of you.
  • Double messaging after no reply. If they don’t reply, let it go and say hi to someone new.

After they reply

Answer what they said, then add a little about yourself and one question back. Conversations die when one person only asks and the other only answers. When it’s going well, suggest a call or meeting somewhere public — and read our dating safety tips first.
